# Interview Script

# **Interview Script**

## **How friends make and follow through on group agreements**

**Interviewer guide:** Allow 20 to 25 minutes. Ask for specific past experiences. Introduce the product only in Part 5\. Follow interesting answers instead of reading every prompt verbatim.

### **1 Opening  2 minutes**

“Hi, I’m Elad. I’m working on a class project about how groups of friends make informal plans and agreements, and what happens when people need to follow through later. I’m trying to understand real experiences, not pitch an app. Would you mind talking for about 20 minutes?”

“Please be honest. If something has never been a problem for you, that’s useful for me to know too.”

### **2 Their group  2 to 3 minutes**

**·**       What groups of friends are you part of? What do you organize or decide together?  
**·**       Where do those conversations usually happen?  
**·**       Tell me about a recent informal agreement your group made.

If they mention a prediction, challenge, or bet, follow that example. Otherwise ask about the most recent playful prediction or challenge. If none comes to mind, do not force the topic.

### **3 One real example  5 to 7 minutes**

**·**       Walk me through what happened, from the first conversation to the end.  
**·**       How did everyone agree on the rules? Were there stakes, such as money, food, a favor, or bragging rights?  
**·**       How did you know who was participating? Did anyone write the details down?  
**·**       How was the result decided, and what happened afterward?  
**·**       Did anyone forget, disagree, or need a reminder? What did you do?

If they organized it, ask what work fell on them. If they participated, ask what they relied on the organizer to do. Return to details: “What happened next?” and “Can you show me how you kept track?”

### **4 Current habits  3 to 4 minutes**

**·**       How often does something like this happen in your group?  
**·**       What tools or habits do you use now? Have you tried anything else?  
**·**       What works well? What gets awkward or forgotten?  
**·**       Have you lost money or time, chased someone, or felt tension because of it? Tell me about the last time.

If the example caused no real difficulty, ask about adjacent group agreements and record that the original problem may be weak.

### **5 Brief concept  3 to 4 minutes**

“We’re exploring a shared place for friend groups to keep track of their informal bets and challenges. A group manager could invite members, set the question and stakes, and record the result. Everyone could see the history. If money was involved, the app could show who owes what and provide a payment link to the winner.”

**·**       What is your first reaction? Which part would have helped in your last example?  
**·**       Which part seems unnecessary or would make you hesitate?  
**·**       How would your group feel about one manager deciding the result?  
**·**       Would a payment link make settling easier, or add social friction? Why?  
**·**       What do you use now that comes closest to this?

Listen without defending the idea or asking whether they would use it.

### **6 Wrap  2 minutes**

**·**       What did I miss about how your group handles this?  
**·**       Who else regularly organizes these kinds of things and might be willing to talk?  
**·**       May I follow up if I have one or two more questions?

Thank them for being candid.

### **After the interview**

Within one hour, record a short snapshot with: participant and group role; a specific past example; problem mentioned before the concept; exact quote showing pain or lack of it; current workaround and time or money involved; reaction and concern; referrals or follow-up; and anything that surprised you.

# Raw Notes \- Interview \#1

## **Raw Notes — Interview 1**

**Interviewee:** A friend of a friend who is a columbia grad student   
**Setting:** In person on campus  
**Topic:** Informal bets and challenges among friends  
**Interviewer:** Shiyu Zhou

## **Group / context**

* Usually watches NBA games online with friends and talks in a Discord voice channel.  
* They use Discord text chat at the same time for reactions, predictions, memes, etc.  
* “During the playoffs we talk about basketball a lot more because there are games almost every day.”

### **Recent example**

* During the NBA playoffs, the group often makes predictions about which team will win a series.  
* Sometimes they also guess which teams will make the Finals or who will win the championship.  
* “We don’t really bet serious money. It’s usually just for fun.”  
* One time they agreed that the person with the most wrong predictions by the end of the playoffs would buy dinner for the group.  
* “I don’t think we had very detailed rules. We just kind of agreed in the Discord call.”

### **How they kept track**

* People typed their predictions in the Discord chat.  
* “If we wanted to check later, we would just scroll back or search the chat.”  
* Sometimes someone took a screenshot.  
* “There are a lot of messages during the games, so sometimes it takes a while to find the old prediction.”  
* He said people sometimes remembered their original picks differently, but it usually became more of a joke than a serious argument.

### **Follow-through / problems**

* “A lot of the time we don’t even seriously keep score.”  
* Sometimes someone reminds the group who lost, but sometimes everyone just forgets.  
* “The dinner thing is not that serious. If we remember, we do it. If not, nobody is really mad.”  
* He said Discord works fine most of the time.  
* The annoying part is mainly when there are a lot of predictions over several weeks and people want to check old messages.

### **Current solution**

* Discord voice channel \+ Discord text chat.  
* Occasionally screenshots important messages.  
* Venmo only if the bet involves actual money.  
* Has never paid for an app or tool to manage friend-group bets.  
* “For us, Discord is usually good enough.”

### **Reaction to concept**

After I described a shared place where friend groups could record bets/challenges, rules, results, and payments:

* “I can see it being useful if a group does this a lot.”  
* “It would be easier than searching through Discord if there were a lot of different bets.”  
* “But if it’s just a simple bet between two people, I probably wouldn’t use a separate app.”  
* “For two people, you can just text each other or remember it.”  
* He thought it made more sense for larger groups or something that lasts across multiple games/rounds.  
* He was unsure about having one manager decide the result.  
* “If the manager is also part of the bet, I don’t know if they should be the only person deciding who won.”  
* Payment links could be useful for money bets, but he did not think they mattered much for casual things like dinner.

### **Wrap-up**

* “Most of our bets are more about bragging rights than actually getting something.”  
* He said the app would probably be more useful for groups that make frequent sports predictions than for occasional casual bets.  
* Said one of his friends follows sports very closely and might also be willing to talk.
