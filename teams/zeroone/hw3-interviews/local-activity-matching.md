# Interview Scripts

# **Interview Script**

## **Finding People to Join Local Activities**

**Interviewer guide:** Allow around 20–25 minutes. Ask about specific past experiences. Do not introduce the product idea until Part 5\. Follow interesting answers instead of reading every question word for word.

### **1\. Opening — 2 minutes**

“Hi, I’m Shiyu. I’m working on a class project about how people make plans and find others to do activities with. I’m trying to understand real experiences, not pitch an app. Would you mind talking for about 20 minutes?”

“Please be honest. If this has never really been a problem for you, that’s useful for me to know too.”

### **2\. Their background and social habits — 2–3 minutes**

* How long have you lived in this city?  
* When you first moved here, did you already know many people?  
* What kinds of things do you usually like to do with other people?  
* Who do you normally ask when you want someone to join you?

### **3\. One real example — 5–7 minutes**

* “Think back to the last 2 or 3 months. Can you tell me about a specific time when you wanted to do something but didn’t have anyone to go with?”  
  * If they need an example: “It could be playing sports, getting food, going to an event, shopping, visiting somewhere, or anything else.”

Then follow up:

* What did you want to do?  
* How did you try to find someone?  
* Did you eventually find someone?  
* What happened if you couldn’t?  
* What was the hardest or most annoying part?  
* Why was that difficult?  
* Has this happened more than once?  
* Was this more common when you first moved here?

### **4\. Current solutions — 3–4 minutes**

* What do you usually do now when you want someone to join you?  
* What works well about that?  
* What doesn’t work well?  
* Have you ever decided not to do something because you couldn’t find anyone?  
* Have you tried any apps, groups, clubs, or communities for this before?  
* Have you ever spent money trying to solve this or make it easier to meet people for activities?

If yes:

* What did you spend money on?  
* About how much?  
* Did it actually help?

### **5\. Brief concept — 3–4 minutes**

“We’re exploring a platform that helps people find others nearby who want to do the same activity at a similar time.”

“For example, someone could post that they want to play tennis, get hotpot, go to a museum, attend an event, or explore somewhere in the city, and other people who are interested and available could join.”

Then ask:

* What’s your first reaction?  
* Would this have helped in the example you just told me about?  
* What seems unnecessary?  
* What would make you hesitate to use something like this?  
* Would you be comfortable meeting people you don’t already know?  
* What would make you feel more comfortable?  
* What do you use now that comes closest to this?

If they say they would use it:

* What situation would you actually use it for?  
* When was the last time you had that exact situation?

### **6\. Wrap-up — 2 minutes**

* What did I miss about how you find people to do things with?  
* Are there certain activities where finding someone is much harder than others?  
* Do you know anyone else who might have dealt with this?  
* Would you be willing to introduce me to them?  
* Would you be open to a short follow-up later?

# Raw Notes \#1

## **Raw Notes — Interview 1**

**Interviewee:** Columbia international grad student, recently moved to New York   
**Setting:** In person off campus  
**Topic:** Finding people to join local activities  
**Interviewer:** Shiyu Zhou

### **Background**

* Recently came to the U.S. for school.  
* Did not know many people when first arriving in New York.  
* Most of the people they knew were classmates, but they were not very close yet.  
* Usually asks classmates or people from school if they want to do something together.

### **One real example**

* Last week, the school had a boat cruise event.  
* “I wanted to go because it sounded fun, but at that time I didn’t really know anyone.”  
* They were worried about going to the meeting point alone and then being at the event without anyone they knew.  
* “I didn’t really want to take the train there by myself and then just stand there alone.”  
* They asked a few classmates if they wanted to go together, but some were busy and some already had plans.  
* “I remember thinking that if nobody wanted to go with me, I might just not go.”  
* They said the main issue was not the event itself. It was more about not having anyone to go with and feeling awkward or lonely.

### **What happened**

* Eventually, one classmate said they were also interested.  
* They took the train to the event together.  
* “Once I knew someone was going with me, I felt much better.”  
* They stayed together for most of the event.  
* They said if they had not found anyone, there was a good chance they would have skipped the event.

### **Current solution**

* Usually asks classmates or people they already know.  
* Sometimes checks WeChat student groups or other school group chats.  
* Has joined school events to meet more people.  
* Has not used an app specifically to find someone to go to an activity with.  
* “Usually I just ask people I know first.”  
* “If everyone is busy, sometimes I just don’t go.”  
* Has not spent money on an app or service to solve this problem.

### **Reaction to concept**

After I described a platform where people nearby could find others who want to do the same activity:

* “I think this would have been useful when I first came here.”  
* “For something like the boat cruise, I probably would have checked if someone else also wanted to go.”  
* They said it would be more useful when someone is new to a city and does not have many friends yet.  
* “Now that I know more people, I probably wouldn’t need it as often.”  
* They would be more comfortable meeting another student than a completely random person.  
* “I would want to know who the person is before meeting them.”  
* They said joining a small group would probably feel more comfortable than meeting one stranger alone.

### **Wrap-up**

* “I think the main thing is just not wanting to go somewhere alone.”  
* They said this problem happened more often during their first few months in New York.  
* They thought events, restaurants, and other social activities are the situations where having someone to go with matters more.  
* Said they know another international student who might also be willing to talk.  
* Open to follow-up questions.
